Now

I build and evaluate agent systems, and I ship things. Most recently thirteen games — designed, built and released end to end, with an agent fleet and no coding background when I started.

I came to AI systems engineering out of operations, quality assurance and management going back to 2003: manufacturing lines, hotels, film, retail, food service. That background is the reason I'm useful here rather than a detour on the way. Evaluating whether a system actually does what it claims is the same discipline whether the output is a moulded part, a hotel night audit, or an agent's answer.

Taking short contracts in agent evaluation.

Through-lines

Hired at the bottom, promoted fast

The pattern repeats across two decades and most of the employers above: entry-level hire, rapid promotion into management. It is also my answer to the degree question — the record shows what happens after someone lets me in the door.

Two and three jobs at once, for years

I drove rideshare concurrently with Hotel Boulderado and CinemaRaven for roughly four years — three jobs at a time, and both ends of the power dynamic at once: managing staff by day, driving for an algorithm at night.

Quality assurance, twice over

Formal QA in two industries. The first was a manufacturing line in 2007 — continuous in-process inspection of my own output plus a formal sampling pass against spec twice per shift. The second was four years testing games. A third verification thread runs through hotel night audit at two properties.

The management arc starts long before the hotel years

Retail supervision from 2004 — six years before the hotel. The management record runs longer, and starts earlier, than a résumé arranged by job title suggests.
